# Titanfall (2014) and Titanfall 2 (2016)

Legend: [S#] numbered source; "unverified" = not confirmed by a page I read; "inference" = my reasoning. Note: Fandom Titanfall wiki returned HTTP 402 and StrategyWiki 403; the wiki.gg Titanfall wiki turned out to be readable with a normal browser header and now supplies most numbers (sources S12-S20). Numbers from wiki.gg are fan-maintained: treat as good but not official.

## Identity
- Respawn Entertainment. Titanfall 1: Xbox One/PC/360, March 2014, multiplayer only, 6v6 with AI, 15 maps, five modes [S2]. Metacritic 86/86/83; 10M sold claimed by IGN Oct 2015 (units vs players unclear) [S2]. Up to 50 active characters per match; AI work was offloaded to cloud servers [S2].
- Titanfall 2: 28 Oct 2016, about 90 developers, two-year cycle, Metacritic 86 PC / 89 PS4 / 87 XB1, campaign about 5 hours per one critic [S1][S3]. Launched between Battlefield 1 and Call of Duty: Infinite Warfare; UK first-week sales about a quarter of the original's; EA had expected 9-10M year one [S1]. Built on a heavily modified Source engine [S1].

## Core loop
- TF1: six humans per team plus up to 12 AI per team; roughly 50 active characters per match; Titan call-in timer shortens as you kill [S2]. Critic note: the timer made every mode a deathmatch [S2].
- TF2 campaign: alternate pilot platforming/shooting with BT-7274 titan sections. Levels: The Pilot's Gauntlet, BT-7274, Blood and Rust, Into The Abyss, Effect and Cause, The Beacon, Trial By Fire, The Ark, The Fold Weapon [S3]. BT's loadouts are swapped by scavenging gear in each level [S1].
- TF2 multiplayer: Titan meter fills slowly with time and faster from kills, damage dealt and objective play; when full you call your Titan, which can crush enemies below it on landing; inside a Titan a full meter means the Core ability [S1]. Boosts (replaced Burn Cards) activate at set meter percentages (see table below) [S16].
- Campaign highlights:
  - Effect and Cause: the player flips between present and past with a time device, changing the same room; critics compared it to Portal and called it one of the most imaginative levels [S1][S3].
  - Into the Abyss: used by the designers as the case study for "action blocks", short rapid prototypes made in game-jam style sprints to find fun before building a level [S21]. Level design had large open spaces for Titan combat and dense routes for pilot traversal, plus platforming blocks that train the movement kit [S1].
  - The campaign teaches mechanics gradually and was meant as a stepping stone to multiplayer [S1].

## Movement
| Mechanic | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Double jump / jump kit | all pilots; the jump kit also softens falls to a safe level | [S1][S19] |
| Wall-run | TF1: about double sprint speed; lasts 1.75 s, or 3.5 s with the Enhanced Parkour kit; start by jumping at a wall then moving along it | [S14] |
| Slide | new in TF2; crouch while moving; slide-hop keeps momentum (jump just before landing then slide again) | [S3][S26] |
| Grapple | tactical in TF2 | [S1] |
| Vault, zipline | TF1; ziplines also launch the jump kit | [S2][S12] |
| Rodeo | TF1: climb onto enemy titan, strip plating, damage hull; TF2: pull out a battery (fixed damage) or drop a frag into the empty socket if you already hold one; doomed titan dies | [S2][S20] |
| Eject | pilot leaves damaged titan | [S2] |
| Titan dash | titans have dash charges instead of jumping (Atlas two, Vanguard one) | [S17] |
- Wall-run duration for TF2, slide speed, jump height, sprint speed: unverified (no official numbers found). A student project quotes Quake-unit constants for a "Titanfall-inspired" controller (sprint wish speed 530 u/s, ground jump 380 u/s, air jump 340 u/s, slide-hop jump 280 u/s, 0.10 s minimum between jumps); these are that project's own tuning, not game data [S25]. A pre-launch tech test note says developers planned faster air and wall-run speed and less damping on bunny-hopping [S24]. Design intent per secondary analysis: preserve speed whenever the player does a "flowing" action [S9; low quality source]. Apex lineage: freeform movement was later scaled back for a no-respawn game, slide extended with downhill speed gain [S8]. Pilot health is much lower in TF2 giving shorter time-to-kill [S3].

## Weapons and equipment
- TF1: ten customizable pilot weapons listed, Smart Pistol Mk5 locks onto multiple targets, about three shots to kill [S2].
- TF2 at launch: 20 pilot primaries and 8 secondaries, 23 primaries after Tricks and Treats update; DLC added six maps, six executions, six kits [S3].
- Pilot weapon families in the series (wiki.gg index): handguns (B3 Wingman, P2011/P2016 Hammond pistols, RE-45 Autopistol, Smart Pistol Mk5/Mk6); SMGs (Alternator, C.A.R., R-97, R-99, Prowler); rifles (G2A4, G2A5, G7 Scout, Hemlok BF-R, R-101C, R-201, R-301, Flatline); LMGs (L-STAR, Spitfire, Devotion); snipers/DMRs (Longbow, Double Take, Kraber-AP); shotguns (EVA-8, Mastiff, Peacekeeper, Mozambique); Bocek bow; explosives (Frag, Arc Grenade, Firestar, Gravity Star, Electric Smoke grenade, Satchel, Thermite); melee (Data Knife, Machete, Pulse Blade) [S28]. Some are later-game or Apex additions, so not all are in each game.
- Anti-titan weapons exist for pilots (Archer, Charge Rifle, Sidewinder, Mag Launcher; names from Frontier Defense page) [S13]. Damage values: unverified.
- Titan primaries: Splitter Rifle, Thermite Launcher, Plasma Railgun, Leadwall, 40mm Tracker Cannon, Predator Cannon, XO-16A2 Chaingun; core weapons: Laser, Flame, Flight, Sword, Salvo, Smart, Burst; ordnance: Arc Wave, Cluster Missile, Firewall, Tracking Rockets, Laser Shot, Power Shot, Rocket Salvo [S3][S17].
- Executions (melee finishers) exist in TF2 [S3].

## Abilities and items
- Pilot tacticals: TF1 has three (Cloak, Stim, Active Radar Pulse). TF2 has seven: Cloak, Pulse Blade (throwable, sonar, instant kill on pilots and minions), Grapple (also pulls enemies), Stim (speed and regeneration), A-Wall (particle wall that blocks incoming and amps outgoing shots), Phase Shift (invulnerable, invisible, about 2 s), Holo Pilot (decoy that mimics your last move) [S19]. Tactical choice sets suit weight light/medium/heavy [S4]. Campaign adds a time-travel device [S16].
- Titan classes: Atlas/Ogre/Stryder in TF1 [S2]; their cores: Damage Core (Atlas, all attacks do 2x), Shield Core (Ogre), Dash Core (Stryder, effectively unlimited dashes); in TF1 a core takes 200 s alone to charge, shortened by damage and kills; 2 s priming [S18]. Titan call-in is the equivalent timer [S2].

## Modes and match options
- TF1: Attrition, Pilot Hunter, Hardpoint Domination, Last Titan Standing, Capture the Flag; Frontier Defense added later in Game Update 8 [S2][S13]. All modes team-based, no free-for-all [S2].
- TF2 (about 11 standard modes): Attrition, Pilots vs Pilots, Bounty Hunt, Amped Hardpoint, CTF, Coliseum (1v1), Live Fire, Last Titan Standing, Skirmish, Titan Brawl, Free for All, Frontier Defense; many featured-only variants such as Tactikill Attrition (killing a pilot refreshes tacticals) [S12].
- Attrition scoring (wiki.gg, resolves the earlier conflict): first team to 300 points (650 in TF2). Grunt 1, Spectre/Stalker 1, Reaper 3, Pilot 5, Titan 5 (10 in TF2), pilot and Titan together 10 (15 in TF2). Before the Tricks and Treats update TF2 Attrition was 10 minutes or 500 points [S12]. Reapers and Stalkers appear later in TF2 Attrition [S12].
- Bounty Hunt: earn cash from kills and bank it at set times (details unverified beyond name) [S13].
- Frontier Defense (4-player PvE, defend a Harvester) [S13]:
  - TF1: between 4 and 6 waves per map (some have 2-3), per-map fixed waves, two retries when the Harvester dies, hackable turrets, star rating by kills and deaths. Enemy types: Grunts and Spectres with unusual weapons, Sniper Spectre (cloaked), Suicide Spectre, Cloak Drone, regular Titans, Nuke Titan (Ogre, explodes at the Harvester), Arc Titan (Stryder, drains shields, blocks rodeo), Mortar Titan (Atlas, long-range bombard), Goblin dropships (shootable; 100 points).
  - TF2 (July 2017 Operation Frontier Shield): exactly 5 waves per map, random map from a pool of 8 maps (Black Water Canal, Forwardbase Kodai, Homestead, Rise, War Games, later Angel City, Drydock, Exoplanet); five difficulty levels (Easy, Normal, Hard, Master, Insane featured); cash earned per kill, wave bonus 700 on easy/normal and 600 higher plus 100 each for Flawless Wave, Wave MVP, No Deaths; Armory buys between waves: Amped Weapons 100, Amped Battery 400, Nuke Rodeo 500, Arc Trap 650, Sentry Turret 1,200, Harvester Shield Booster 1,200; Aegis ranks unlock Titan upgrades. Enemy changes: Spectres removed, Stalkers (unarmed suicide walkers), Mortar Spectres, Reapers that spawn Ticks, Plasma Drones, Shield Captain Grunts on Master and Insane, Sniper/Arc/Nuke/Mortar Titans built on Tone/Ronin/Legion chassis.

## Map design
- TF2 team said the first game was too chaotic, so the pace was slowed and each map got more verticality (the lead called it "3D swiss cheese") and a "window pane" layout with three obvious routes (left, middle, right) for predictability; brighter palette; remastered TF1 maps returned [S1].
- Wall-run lines, rooftops, interior pockets, titan-scale streets, ziplines; campaign levels mix wall-run puzzles with open Titan arenas [S1][S21]. Specific map dimensions not in fetched sources.
- Inference: pilot lanes (walls, rooftops) and titan lanes (open streets) overlap; ensure 3-4 m wide gaps for wall-runs, and give every lane at least three route choices.

## Bots and AI
- Minions: human Grunts and robotic Spectres, up to 12 AI per team, serve as cannon fodder and score fodder [S2]. Grunts weak, only dangerous in numbers [S6]. Spectres can be hacked with the Data Knife [S22]. Reapers (heavy; 3,500 hp) hold areas, move in bursts, barrage with rocket launchers then leap away with thrusters, appear later in a match in Attrition, crush on landing, weak at cooling vents [S6]. Critics found TF1 grunts too unintelligent [S2].
- Design takeaway for bots: minions are meant to be easy kills that feed meter and score and populate the world; real threat comes from pilot-bots and titans. Inference.
- Pilot-bot recipe (inference, using Halo task idea [S10]): states hunt/flank/wallrun-traverse/rodeo/embark; use waypoint graph with wall-run edges; titan-bot follows owner or attacks. Auto-Titan has Follow and Guard modes when the pilot disembarks [S29].
- Spawning: Reapers land where respawning titans drop [S6].

## Progression
- TF1: XP unlocks weapons and perks; level 50 can "regenerate" to 1 for prestige; burn card packs at levels 25, 30, 40, 45, 50 [S2][S15].
- TF2: unlock weapons, kits, camos; Monarch needs level 20; Frontier Defense uses Aegis ranks; the progression was criticised as thin by one reviewer [S1][S17].

## HUD and options
- Titan call-in timer/meter; boost icon at percent thresholds [S1]; minimap with AI dots; options unverified. Rodeo alert at top of HUD when a pilot boards your Titan [S20].

## Best features to keep
1. TF2 momentum movement (slide-hop, wall-run, grapple) [S3][S8].
2. Campaign pacing with a single gimmick per level, e.g. Effect and Cause time swap [S1].
3. Titan battery and doomed second-health-bar to keep fights decisive [S3].
4. Minions give a populated field and easy points [S2].
5. Boosts replacing single-life burn cards.
6. Titan classes with a clear silhouette and a fixed kit (one primary, ordnance, defensive, tactical, core) [S1][S17].
7. Frontier Defense: fixed waves per map with an in-run shop [S13].

## Mistakes to avoid
- TF1 had no campaign, few modes, no private matches [S2].
- TF2 launch between big shooters hurt sales [S1].
- Short campaign (about 5 h) and thin story per several reviewers [S1].
- Timer-based titan access made every mode deathmatch [S2].
- Low-quality AI grunts [S2].
- TF2 multiplayer progression called lacking in substance, multiplayer unforgiving, too few AI modes, some design changes "odd" [S1].
- TF1 campaign-multiplayer was an extended tutorial with little story [S2].

## Adaptation notes
- Offline bots: implement Grunts as cheap capsule agents with basic LOS shooting, 2-3 shot HP (original 100/200 HP); spawn waves up to cap of 12 per side.
- Titan: single large mesh with simple weapon sets; embark/disembark as state toggle; rodeo as proximity hold-key for battery steal.
- Movement: tune for low end: server-less deterministic physics, fixed 120 Hz step, wall detection via few raycasts per frame; wall-run timer 1.75 s as a starting value.
- Touch: slide and jump buttons, wall-run auto when airborne next to wall.
- Gamepad: standard dual-stick with aim assist.
- Attrition for bots: score table above (1/1/3/5/5-10) with a 300 or 650 target.
