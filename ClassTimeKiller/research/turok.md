# Turok: Dinosaur Hunter (1997, plus Nightdive remaster) with notes from Turok 2 (1998) and Turok (2008)

Method note: the 1997 PC manual, the Turok 2 PC manual (OCR text, partly garbled) and the 2008 Xbox 360 manual (OCR text, partly garbled) were all read from archive.org. Wikipedia pages for the games were read in full. The Turok wiki, GameFAQs, StrategyWiki and Giant Bomb stayed blocked (403). Per-weapon damage numbers are not in any manual; they are marked unverified. Source numbers refer to the list at the end.

## Identity
- Iguana Entertainment, 1997, N64 and PC; Nightdive remaster PC 2015, Xbox One 2018, Switch later [8]. About 1.5 million sold by 1998 [1]. Metacritic console 85 [1]. Seven main levels plus hub (manual says eight large levels, Hub reached from Level 1) [3]. Goal: assemble the eight-piece Chronoscepter [1][3].
- Turok 2: Seeds of Evil: Iguana, December 1998 (N64), PC 1999; six large levels plus hub; Metacritic about 86 (N64); 1.4 million sold by January 1999; about 21 months of development; PC port scored 73% on GameRankings [5][11]. Hero is Joshua Fireseed; villain is the Primagen [11].
- Turok (2008): Propaganda Games, Unreal Engine 3, Xbox 360/PS3/PC; Wikipedia lists a GameTrailers 8.3/10 and a 1UP C+ [7][12].

## Core loop
- Explore large open levels, find hidden keys and one Chronoscepter piece per level; bring keys back to the Hub, where each gateway unlocks when all its keys are placed [3]. Floor tiles at each level entrance show found keys and the piece [3]. Revisit earlier levels with new explosives to reach new areas [3]. Hunting deer and boar gives health [3]. Dropped items disappear quickly, so fight at close range [1].
- Fights: enemies approach in mixed groups; ammo is found on bodies [3].
- Turok 2 loop: each level has several objectives (destroy objects, rescue hostages, defeat enemies), then a portal exit; levels 1-3 end with protecting a totem, levels 4-5 with totem then boss, level 6 with boss only [5]. A Hub Portal teleports between levels; keys collected in a level open hub doors for others [11].
- Turok 2008 loop: linear story chapters, mixed fights against soldiers and neutral dinosaurs, context-sensitive button prompts for dino mauling and quiet knife kills [12].

## Movement
- Hold jump longer for a higher jump; diagonal jumps go farther [3]. Swimming: only the knife works, hold jump to surface [3]. Climbing: walk into climbable surfaces, jump to leap off; no weapons while climbing [3]. Run/walk toggle [3]. Reviewers: first-person platforming works poorly because you cannot see your feet [1]. Remaster has smoother controls and fully rebindable input [8].
- Turok 2: climb ladders, vines and marked walls, use only certain weapons while climbing; swimming with a limited breath (the manual says Turok blacks out and loses a life if he cannot surface); an underwater weapon wheel; a Styracosaurus mount with a 20 mm cannon and artillery in some levels [11]. Talisman tiles grant special moves: Firewalk (cross lava), Whispers (ride updrafts), Eyes of Truth (reveal invisible paths), Leap of Faith (long jumps), Breath of Life (walk the River of Souls) [11]. Quick-dodge on 2008 [12].

## Abilities and items
- Health pickups: small, full, ultra (can exceed 100), mortal wound (from knife/arrow kills, +5 max health) [3]. Life Force: yellow = 1, purple = 10, 100 points = extra life [3]. Tek Armor absorbs damage then breaks [3]. Backpack increases ammo capacity [3]. Spiritual Invincibility: Turok super fast, enemies slow [3].
- Turok 2 health: silver +2 (can pass 100 without raising the cap), blue +10, orange full, gold ultra (+100 temporary), Life Force gold +1 and red +10; save portals once per level restore health and ammo [11].

## Health and damage
- Health 100, number display; zero health costs a life; last life ends the game [1][3]. PC port kept checkpoint saves, criticised [1]. Lives top left, Life Force top right [3].
- Difficulty: the 1997 PC manual shows no difficulty choice (unverified for the N64). Turok 2 PC has Easy, Normal, Hard and an Auto Aim toggle [11]. Turok 2008 has Normal and Hard; Inhuman unlocks after beating either [12]. What each level changes numerically: unverified.

## Modes and match options
- Original N64 had no multiplayer; PC port also lacked it [1]. Turok 2 N64: up to 4-player splitscreen, Deathmatch, Team Deathmatch, and Frag Tag, where one random player becomes a harmless monkey with little health and must reach a set point to turn back and pass the curse on; others score by killing the monkey and the monkey loses points by dying [5][14]. Turok 2 PC (up to 16 players): Rok Match (deathmatch), Team Rok Match, Arena (1v1 with a queue, health and ammo restored for the winner, no pickups), Team Arena, and Capture the Flag; 11 playable characters with different speed, health and attack (an "all characters same" option exists); friendly-fire, weapons-stay and enemies-drop-weapons toggles; frag, time and point limits [11]. Raptor is close-range only but very fast [5].
- Turok: Rage Wars (1999, same engine) names the modes Bloodlust and Team Bloodlust (deathmatch with suicide penalty), Capture the Flag, and Monkey Tag (the Frag Tag idea; target can also be a chicken, baby raptor or robot) [13]. Monkey Tag and Bloodlust are therefore Rage Wars names; Turok 2 itself used Frag Tag [13][14].
- Turok 2 remaster adds Last Turok Standing [5]. Turok 2008: up to 16 players, Team Deathmatch, CTF, Wargames, Assault CTF, three co-op missions for four; random AI dinosaurs and insects (up to 4 on a map) attack all sides; playlists are small/large free-for-all, small/large team game and co-op; ranked, player and private matches [7][12].
- Cheat codes exist (invincibility, all weapons, level warp) [10]; Turok 2 has a cheats menu where entered cheats are stored with on/off [11].

## Map design
- Hub of arched gateways around a central platform; Levels 2-7 around it, final level center [3]. Terrain: jungle, underwater caverns, catacombs, ancient cities, the Campaigner's fortress [4].
- Turok 2: six large levels, a hub with six portals and a gate to the final boss; all hub portals except the first need keys, and level 1 holds the keys for levels 2 and 3, level 2 for 4, level 3 for 5, levels 4 and 5 for 6, allowing partial non-linear order [5]. Each level also has a Primagen Key that needs a talisman chamber and a feather; the six keys open the boss gate; so levels are reentered several times [5][11]. Automap present [5][11]. Designers wanted exploration and objectives inspired by Mario and Zelda, and reduced and disguised the fog as atmosphere, so players see about five times farther than in the first game (the article's claim) [5].
- Reviewers: levels so complex that players "run around in circles" [5]; confusing level design on PC; forced replay of a level when a key is missed [5].
- Multiplayer levels were built for running, jumping, climbing and swimming, giving many escape routes [5].

## Bots and AI
- Enemies from the 1997 manual [3]: Ancient Warriors (blowguns, die to shotguns), Raptors (close range), Leapers (erratic jumps), Pur-Lins (slow, knock you off cliffs, some fireball blasters), Alien Infantry (accurate photon blasters, among deadliest), Subterraneans (burrow then spit), Killer Plants, Triceratops with twin rocket launchers, Demons, Sludge Beasts, Cyborgs (resist arrows and shotguns).
- Turok 2 enemies from the PC manual [11]: Endtrails (tyrannosaur descendants, ion gauntlets at range, claws close), Hunters (leaping, bred as hounds), Sentinels (blade-armed flesh-eater grunts), Blind Ones (eyeless, cave spiders, die in sunlight), Purr-Linn (agile, jump, throw boulders), Mantid Soldiers (very tough armored insects), Deadmen (get up again after being shot, even when cut in half), Primagen Troopers (laser pistols and bayonets). Reviewers praised advanced enemy AI; enemies take cover and pop up to throw explosives [5].
- Turok 2008: dinosaurs are neutral and attack anyone; flares and the shotgun flare lure them onto soldiers; stealth knife kills; mauling prompts; aim assist considered inadequate [7][12].

## Progression
- Keys unlock gateways, pieces unlock the ending, weapon pickups scattered in levels; cheat-free progression is item-driven [3]. Turok 2: talismans gate Primagen Keys [5][11]. Turok 2008 locks chapters until beaten [12].

## HUD and options
- Health and ammo at the bottom, lives and Life Force on top [3]. Distance fog hides pop-in on N64 [1]. Remaster: widescreen, high resolution, rebindable controls, dynamic lighting [8]. Auto-map toggle exists [3]. Turok 2 options: Auto Aim, blood color (red, green, off), HUD opacity, separate horizontal and vertical sensitivity, quick or normal weapon select, save up to 16 games [11]. 2008 HUD: damage arrow, reticle turns red over enemies [12].

## Best features to keep
- Hub with visual key tracker on the floor tiles: clear goals [3].
- Wide weapon progression from knife/bow to absurd weapons (Fusion Cannon spares the player, Chronoscepter finale) [3][4].
- Charge/hold weapons and ammo-type priority [3].
- Pickups that reward playstyle (mortal wound for silent kills, deer for health) [3].
- Cerebral-Bore style gimmick weapons, upgrade toggles (Shotgun to Shredder) [5][11].
- Frag Tag / Monkey Tag: a cheap asymmetric party mode [5][13].
- Neutral wildlife that can be lured onto enemies [12].

## Mistakes to avoid
- Heavy fog and frame-rate dips on N64; visibility limited to a small radius; IGN called Turok 2 choppy and its PC fog uncommon for the time [1][5].
- Getting lost: complex levels and confusing layout; replaying a level to fetch a missed key [5].
- First-person platforming without feet visibility [1].
- Awkward controls, limited keyboard support, checkpoint-only saves in the PC port (Turok 2 PC lets you save anywhere) [1][5].
- Dated models and minimal story in the remaster [8].
- Turok 2008: bad stealth, camera shake, glitches, weak aim assist, frustrating level design; the cancelled "Grab Bag" achievement rewarded team killing [7].

## Adaptation notes
- Hub-and-gateway map generator: a central room with N doors; each door opens when you collect K key tokens in its level. A floor panel shows found keys. Optionally gate bonus keys behind a talisman power found in another level, as Turok 2 does.
- Replace fog with a compass or objective arrow to avoid being lost; draw distance on integrated GPUs can still use light fog. Add an auto-map.
- Platforming: keep jumps forgiving; add a shadow blob or a ground marker to compensate for no feet.
- Versus bots: arena of raptor-like melee swarms plus soldier-like shooters, ammo-type priority, life-force score to extra life; Frag Tag as a mode (random bot or player becomes a weak target that must reach a marker).
- Neutral predators (2008 idea): wildlife that attacks the nearest actor, lured by a flare item.
- Few detailed numbers on per-weapon damage found: unverified.
