# BioShock (2007), with BioShock 2 and Infinite notes

Tags [n] refer to Sources; "unverified" = author recollection or not on a fetched page. "(snippet)" in Sources = only a search-result excerpt was read.

## Identity
- First-person shooter with RPG elements in the underwater city of Rapture; spiritual successor to System Shock 2 (Levine and ex-Looking Glass staff) [1][3].
- Pillars: weapons + plasmids used together, environment as ally, moral Little Sister choice, Big Daddy ecology.
- Budget/scale context: about 25 million USD, 93 developers at peak; Metacritic 96 on PC and Xbox 360 [1].

## Core loop
- Explore district, scavenge ammo/cash/EVE, fight Splicers, hack, research enemies with the camera, find a Big Daddy, choose to harvest or rescue its Little Sister, spend ADAM on plasmids/tonics/slots at Gatherer's Gardens [1][9][13].
- Harvest = max ADAM but kills the girl; rescue = less ADAM, Tenenbaum compensates with gifts; Polygon's Friedman said rescue costs only about 10% of possible ADAM [1]. Levine himself said he wanted the rescue reward minimal so it felt like a harder path, but the publisher resisted [17].
- BioShock 2 lets you adopt a Little Sister and escort her to corpses while defending her; harvesting or saving all sisters on a level provokes a Big Sister hunt [2][7].

## Movement
- Standard FPS walk/jump, no sprint in the original (unverified). Infinite adds Sky-Line rail riding with one-handed weapons and a hook melee [3].

## Weapons and equipment
- Original weapons: wrench, pistol, machine gun, shotgun, grenade launcher, chemical thrower, crossbow, plus the research camera (not a real weapon) [9].
- Each weapon except the wrench can be upgraded twice at Power to the People machines; 12 machines in total, each single use [9]. Upgrades add range, less recoil and unlock ammo types [1].
- Ammo per weapon [9]:
  | Weapon | Standard | Special types |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pistol | .38 rounds | armor-piercing, anti-personnel |
  | Machine gun | standard | anti-personnel auto, armor-piercing auto |
  | Shotgun | standard | Electric Buck, Exploding Buck |
  | Grenade launcher | grenades | proximity mines, heat-seeking RPGs |
  | Chemical thrower | napalm-style fuel (unverified name) | liquid nitrogen, electric gel |
  | Crossbow | standard bolts (unverified) | incendiary, trap bolts |
- Damage numbers per shot, ammo carry caps: unverified (not found on any reachable page).
- Matchups: electric gel/Electric Buck and armor-piercing rounds recommended against Big Daddies; armor-piercing and electricity hurt bots and turrets [9].
- Weapons and plasmids use one hand slot in the original, so switching is not instant; a reviewer called that a strength because fights need planning [14].
- BioShock 2: dual wield weapon + plasmid; three cumulative weapon upgrades unlock a special effect (rivet gun ignites enemies) [2].
- Infinite: only two weapons carried [3].

## Abilities and items
- Plasmids use EVE; tonics are passive; limited slots; swapped at stations; ADAM buys both [1].
- Base plasmid list (original) [9][13]:
  | Plasmid | Effect | Upgrades | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Electro Bolt | stuns/paralyzes, extra damage on stunned target, shocks water | 2, 3 | ranked best by two guides [9][13] |
  | Incinerate! | sets targets and objects on fire, melts ice | 2, 3 | strong on oil stains; countered by water [5][13] |
  | Winter Blast | freezes enemies/items; shattered frozen enemies drop no loot | 2, 3 | frozen hacked objects also possible [13] |
  | Telekinesis | pick up and throw objects, grab out-of-reach items | none listed | useful against missile turrets [13] |
  | Cyclone Trap | vortex throws enemies into the air | 2 | [9][13] |
  | Insect Swarm | swarm panics a group | 2, 3 | called rarely useful [13] |
  | Enrage | target attacks its allies | none listed | [13] |
  | Hypnotize Big Daddy | Big Daddy becomes friendly for a while | 2 | Little Sister is then left unguarded [7][9] |
  | Target Dummy | decoy | none listed | [9][13] |
  | Security Bullseye | marks a target for security systems | none listed | mostly used on Big Daddies [13] |
- DLC plasmids (Ryan Industries patch): Sonic Boom (force blast), plus tonics EVE Saver, Vending Expert, Machine Buster (bonus damage to machines) [10].
- EVE cost per cast: unverified (no fetched page lists the numbers). Verified rules: a plasmid still fires with a fraction of the needed EVE; empty EVE auto-injects a hypo unless you switch to a weapon in time; cigarettes, pep bars and coffee restore a little EVE [11].
- ADAM purchase prices exist per tier (BioShock 2 Winter Blast example 60/100/200) [6]; original prices unverified.
- Combos: water + Electro Bolt shocks everything standing in it (achievement for the kill) [9]; fire on oil spills is a recommended area-denial tactic [9]; Incinerate also melts ice and opens paths; Telekinesis throws environmental objects and explosives; Winter Blast then melee/shatter [13]. Water extinguishes fire (unverified detail).
- Gene tonics: three categories in the original: Physical (own attributes), Engineering (hacking/tech), Combat (fighting). Each category has its own slots, starting at 2 each with more bought at Gatherer's Gardens, up to 6 per type (search snippets, sources differ) [4][22]. BioShock 2 dropped the categories: any mix up to a slot cap of 18, starting with 4 [22].
- Tonic examples [4][9][15]: EVE Saver / EVE Link (cheaper plasmids), Static Discharge (stuns melee attackers), Electric Flesh and Human Inferno (resist electric/fire), Armored Shell (reduces damage; one LP author says levels 1+2 stack to 40%), Natural Camouflage, Booze Hound, Bloodlust (wrench hits restore EVE), Medical Expert, Speedy Hack, Safe Cracker, Shorten Alarm, Damage Research, Clever Inventor.
- Research camera: no direct damage. Five research levels per enemy type; levels 1, 3, 5 give a permanent damage bonus, levels 2 and 4 give tonics and other rewards. Score favors centered, close, multi-enemy, action and first-time shots (search snippet) [20].
- Hacking (original): pipe puzzle with a timer, from a start to an end point; overload tiles and alarm tiles stop or punish it; hitting an alarm tile alerts security bots [9]. Tonics remove tiles (Alarm Expert removes 2/4 alarm tiles; Focused Hacker removes 2/4 overload tiles; Hacking Expert 1+1 then 2+2) and an auto-hack tool skips the game [9]. Hacked vending machines sell at about half price; hacked health stations cost 10 instead of 16 and hurt enemies who use them [9].
- Turrets, cameras and security bots: hackable; hacked ones fight for you and a camera can summon help against enemies; Security Expert and turret research make hacking bots/turrets automatic at top level; armor-piercing and electricity damage them [9].
- BioShock 2 hacking: a needle timing mini-game (green = success, blue = bonus, white/red = fail with damage or alarm); real-time, cannot be paused [2].
- Infinite vigors: powers using Salt, can set traps; Elizabeth opens tears and tosses supplies [3].

## Health and damage
- Vita-Chambers: on death Jack revives at the last chamber passed at about half health; enemies keep their current health; none exist in the final fight [1][9].
- v1.1 patch (and Xbox 360 DLC) added an option to disable them; achievements "I Choose the Impossible" and "Brass Balls" require it [12][18].
- Levine: Vita-Chambers kept the flow because players quit on failure, but they removed weight from failure; Infinite's 1999 Mode was meant to restore it [16].
- Infinite: regenerating shield, health from kits; dying costs money and partly heals nearby enemies [3].

## Modes and match options
- Difficulty (original) [12]: Easy (enemies 40% less health, less accurate, slower; plasmids cost half; more loot); Normal; Hard (about double enemy damage, 50% more health, faster, plasmids cost 30% more; less loot); Survivor (almost 150% more health, much faster, much less EVE from Vita-Chamber revive; very little loot). Switchable any time, saving unrestricted. Remastered reportedly resets to Medium on load (forum report, unverified fix) [19].
- BioShock 2 multiplayer "Fall of Rapture": built by Digital Extremes, story set in 1959-60 civil war, a Sinclair Solutions testing program; seven modes including Deathmatch variants "Survival of the Fittest" and "Civil War", "Capture the Sister" (CTF with a Little Sister) and "Turf War" (area control); other three unnamed on the fetched page. Maps from the first game; new chain gun and Aero Dash plasmid; unlocks by leveling; fewer simultaneous weapons/plasmids than single player [2].
- Playtests found team play more fun than rules that changed mid-match [2].
- Clone modes: "Sister Run" (carry the Sister to goal), plasmid DM with environment hazards (water + electro).

## Map design
- Themed districts (Arcadia, Fort Frolic, Medical Pavilion, Hephaestus, Olympus etc.) with distinct art identity and audio diaries for narrative [13]. Hazards: water pools, oil slicks, ice, electric locks, explosive pipes (Poseidon Plaza fight) used for combos [1][9].

## Bots and AI
- Splicers: disfigured by ADAM overuse [14]. Research categories and behaviors [9][20]:
  | Type | Behavior |
  |---|---|
  | Thuggish | melee |
  | Leadhead | gun user |
  | Nitro | throws explosives; research raises dud chance |
  | Spider | drops from ceilings/walls, attacks in groups |
  | Houdini | teleports, throws fire; research eases predicting destination |
- Splicers generally do not attack other AI outside scripted events; Big Daddies do [21].
- Big Daddy (Rosie: rivet gun, ranged; Bouncer: drill, melee) escorts Little Sisters, stays passive unless a sister (or itself) is attacked, then retaliates until one dies; helmet light shows mood: yellow indifferent, red enraged, green hypnotized [7]. BioShock 2 Alpha Series is pair-bonded to one sister and may collapse or berserk if she is lost; Lancer (Minerva's Den, ion laser and flash) and Rumbler (rockets, mini-turrets) are later variants [7].
- Little Sisters gather ADAM from corpses; harvest or rescue after the Big Daddy dies [1][8].
- Big Sister in BioShock 2 hunts the player after sisters on a level are all harvested or saved; redesigned so she can be beaten [2].
- Infinite: Heavy Hitters as mini-bosses; automatons as security [3].
- Bot ideas: ecology bots (protector bot that only attacks when the Sister-carrier is hit), plasmid-using bots, environment-aware bots that avoid fire in water.

## Progression
- ADAM buys plasmids/tonics/slots; money for ammo/vending; upgrade stations for weapons; research camera for damage bonuses; Infinite uses gear slots and infusions [1][3][20].

## HUD and options
- EVE and health bars, plasmid/weapon swap, camera, hack timer. Vita-Chamber toggle added, difficulty changeable mid-game [1][12].

## Best features to keep
- Plasmid + weapon pairing and environmental combos [9][13].
- Research bonus loop [20].
- Big Daddy/Little Sister set piece as optional high-reward fight [7].
- Hackable security as a pickup-and-use ally [9].

## Mistakes to avoid
- Vita-Chambers removing tension [1][16]; weak final boss, with Levine saying six more months could have fixed gun combat and boss lag [1]; one player writeup calls the second half rushed [15].
- Black-and-white moral choice with little real impact (Jonathan Blow, Daniel Friedman); Hocking's "ludonarrative dissonance" critique [1].
- Clumsy, time-consuming pipe hacking, especially on console [14].
- Repeated Sister defense, tedious scavenging, similar splicer fights [2]; multiplayer called unnecessary by some critics and luck-driven by Destructoid [2].
- Infinite: monotonous combat, little escalation, two-weapon limit disliked, lenient death [3].

## Adaptation notes
- Replace Vita-Chamber with limited respawn tokens or timed bot-match respawn.
- Electro/fire/water combos: tag floor decals as "wet" or "oil"; one bit per cell; cheap to implement.
- Research camera as scan-to-learn bots' weaknesses (5 tiers, bonus at tiers 1/3/5 is a ready-made curve).
- Splicer-style bots: 4-5 archetypes with distinct silhouettes (melee, gun, thrower, climber, teleporter).
- Difficulty as multipliers on bot health, damage, accuracy, speed and ability cost, using the Easy/Hard table above as a starting point.
- Hack-a-turret: short timing mini-game with fail alarm.
