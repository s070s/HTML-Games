# Deus Ex (2000), with Human Revolution and Mankind Divided notes

Tags [n] refer to Sources. "unverified" = author recollection, a single weak source, or not on a fetched page. "snippet" = seen only in a search-result excerpt, not a fetched page. Fandom pages were blocked (402/403) and Wayback was refused, so wiki numbers come from search excerpts.

## Identity
- Action RPG / FPS / stealth hybrid; you are JC Denton, a UNATCO agent with nanotech augmentations [1].
- Concept (Warren Spector, first "Troubleshooter") was a real-world RPG focused on choice and consequence [5]; built on licensed Unreal Engine, RPG systems added in-house [1][6].
- Development: Spector started the design document in 1993; pre-production Aug 1997 (about six months, team of six); full production 28 months with about 20 staff; gold June 2000 [1][6]. Planned for Christmas 1998, which the postmortem calls never realistic [6].
- Cut: female JC option, White House, Russia and space-station levels [1]. Multiplayer was cut from the original release for lack of time and added by patch [1][6].
- Sales: over 1 million worldwide by April 2009; Metacritic 90 (PC), 81 (PS2) [1].

## Core loop
- Receive mission, enter hub, pick approach: stealth, hacking, armed assault, conversation, or a mix; the same objective has multiple routes [1][2][5].
- Spector reasoning: a player weak at one style can switch rather than quit [5]; at GDC 2017 he reportedly said players often tried one approach, reloaded and tried others (summary only, unverified) [8].
- Skill points from objectives and exploration spend on 11 skills; augmentation canisters unlock and upgrade augmentations [1].
- Resource loop: biocells refill augmentation energy, medkits heal, lockpicks/multitools are consumed by locks and keypads [1][15][17].

## Movement
- Leg augmentations: Run Silent (silent running when fully upgraded) or Speed Enhancement (more speed and jump height) [13][18]. Energy cost 40 units/min each at the listed base value [19].
- Environmental Training skill raises jump height and resistance to hostile environments (Wikipedia wording) [1]; Swimming skill makes Denton swim faster and use oxygen better [15].
- Crouching reduces noise [16]. Exact speed and jump numbers per augmentation level: unverified.
- Leg injury slows movement: see Health and damage [20].

## Abilities and items
- Start with three augmentations: Infolink, light, friend-or-foe identifier (IFF) [1].
- One augmentation per body slot, installed permanently; each can be upgraded up to four levels with upgrade canisters (medical bots install them); energy is bioelectric and refilled by biocells [1][18][19].
- Slots, each with two mutually exclusive options [18]:
  - Cranial: Aggressive Defense System or Spy Drone.
  - Eyes: Targeting or Vision Enhancement.
  - Torso (3 slots): Aqualung or Environmental Resistance; Regeneration or Energy Shield; Synthetic Heart or Power Recirculator.
  - Arms: Combat Strength or Microfibral Muscle.
  - Subdermal (2 slots): Ballistic Protection or EMP Shield; Cloak or Radar Transparency.
  - Legs: Run Silent or Speed Enhancement.
- Energy cost, units per minute at the listed base value (whether it falls with level is unverified except for cloaks) [19]:

- HR (2011): Praxis Kits, regenerating health, cover (third person), takedowns [3]. MD (2016): experimental augmentations can disable another to avoid overheating, social augmentation, Breach hacking mode [4].

## Health and damage
- Six hit locations in single-player (head, torso, two arms, two legs), each with its own health; heal with medkits, which restore 30 HP at untrained Medicine (60/75/90 at higher levels per snippet) [15][22]; Regeneration heals over time [13].
- Reported effects (source-code reading by one forum poster, not independently verified) [20]:
  - Leg below 67% health: small speed penalty; below 34%: larger; destroyed: largest; both legs destroyed: speed fixed at 80% and forced crouch-walk.
  - Torso below 67%: tiny speed penalty; torso or head incapacitated is fatal.
  - Arm below 67%: aim penalty +0.1; below 34%: +0.2; destroyed: +0.5 (0.0 is perfect).
  - Head below 67%: aim penalty +0.1.
- Multiplayer: three hit locations (legs, torso, head) with the same health pool; torso kill, headshot counts double, destroyed legs slow you and force crouching with no jumping [10].
- Damage reducers: Ballistic Protection and Energy Shield augmentations, armor items, Environmental skill [13][15].
- HR switched to regenerating health, divisive among fans [3].

## Modes and match options
- Single-player campaign only at release; deathmatch and team deathmatch added by post-release patch (five maps from campaign levels) [1][23].
- Mode count disagrees: the version 1.1 readme lists Death Match and Team Death Match; Wikipedia's mods page lists deathmatch, basic team deathmatch, advanced team deathmatch [23].
- Multiplayer rules: carry three weapons, ammo from crates, racks and corpses, alternate ammo only from cabinets; canisters come from kills unless the host changes it; basic TDM gives maximum weapon skills, other types may start lower with skill points [1][10].
- Servers shut April 2014; community patches exist [1][23].
- Mankind Divided Breach: arcade hacking infiltration, timed, leaderboards, no multiplayer [4].
- Clone modes (design ideas): "stealth contract" maps scored by alarms, "augment draft" DM where you pick one augmentation per life.

## Map design
- Hubs (Liberty Island, Hong Kong, Paris, NYC) with several entrances: vents, doors, windows, hacking, water routes [1][5].
- Liberty Island (first real level) was built as a vertical slice of the intended gameplay; Harvey Smith changed the opening from an office arrival to a hostile start after playing Ultima Underworld [2][1].
- Routes: pick sniper rifle, mini-crossbow or GEP gun from Paul; enter the statue by the front door or by crates at the back; kill or talk down the NSF commander. A patrolling robot can be killed with the GEP gun, bypassed via a hacked camera, avoided by the back entrance, or disabled with an EMP grenade [2].
- Rule: each path should support combat, stealth and hacking, with open areas between encounters for quiet moments; long sightlines cost frame rate [2].
- Postmortem (Spector): goal was to key every problem to skills, augmentations, items or weapons with more than one route; the simulation was too shallow so designers brute-forced a skill path, an action path and a conversation path per problem [6]. Real locations proved poor game spaces; fun overrode accuracy [6]. Smith knew room size limits because he built rooms daily [7 per search summary].
- Early test maps (White House, part of Hong Kong) exposed performance limits and led to splitting maps into smaller pieces [6].

## Bots and AI
- AI was built on Unreal Tournament shooter AI, designed in isolation and not prototyped early; it kept changing until the final days, and Spector rates it compelling but inconsistent [6]. Critics call it erratic [1]; MD's AI criticised [4].
- Detection cues documented: noise (crouching lowers it, sniper rifle and baton/crowbar kills are loud unless silenced or non-lethal), patrol routes can be learned and timed, bodies can be picked up and moved, Cloak hides from humans and Radar Transparency hides from machines [16][14][13].
- Security cameras that spot you change the level (alarm response, per Liberty Island article) [2].
- NPC awareness state names (idle, alerted, hunting, fleeing, alarm raising), light-level and exact noise radii: unverified; no source found. Treat the list as a design suggestion.
- HR: bodies can be hidden to avoid raising alarms [3].
- Bot ideas: sound-based alert propagation, locational-damage reactions (leg hit = limp), alarm panels.

## Progression
- Skill points per objective; 5000 bonus points at start (Pistol reportedly starts Trained; one guide notes a refund bug when downgrading) [17][19]. Points can be reassigned only during character creation [1].
- About 34k points exist in the game versus about 87k to master everything (snippet) [22]. Liberty Island alone gave 1690 points in one guide [19].
- Skills (11): Pistol, Rifle, Heavy, Low-Tech, Demolition (damage, aim; Demolition also longer grenade disarm time), Lockpicking, Electronics, Computer, Medicine, Swimming, Environmental Training [1][15][17].
- Levels: untrained, trained, advanced, master. Costs from snippets: Rifle 1575 / 3150 / 5250; Computer 1125 / 2250 / 3750; costs differ per skill [22]. Multiplayer prices: 2000 per weapon upgrade, 1000 per Demolition upgrade [11].
- Effects with numbers (snippets) [22]: Rifle damage bonus reaches +100% at master (inventory display shows half); Electronics multitool reduces keypad strength by 10 / 25 / 40 / 75%; Medicine heals 30 / 60 / 75 / 90 HP per medkit; untrained lockpicks open doors up to 10% strength [19]. Computer: Trained enables hacking, higher levels hack faster and give more ATM credits [15]. Lockpicking and Electronics reduce picks or multitools needed, never below one [15]. Swimming: speed and oxygen use [15].
- Player opinion: Lockpicking, Electronics, Computer solid; Medicine weak with Regeneration; Swimming least useful [21].

## HUD and options
- Inventory grid, augmentation bar with energy and toggles, body silhouette showing limb health (silhouette: unverified for exact look). Bound keys toggle augmentations (e.g. Aggressive Defense on F9 default in MP guide) [12]. No captions verified.

## Best features to keep
- Multiple solutions per objective, with a skill path, action path and talk path [5][6].
- Locational damage and limb status as readable feedback [20].
- Augmentation as a swappable power choice with an energy tradeoff and exclusive pairs per slot [18][19].
- Hostile, readable opening level as tutorial and showcase [2].

## Mistakes to avoid
- Dated combat, erratic AI, stiff animation, low lighting, high system requirements, weak or inconsistent voice acting; GameSpot found combat and hacking shallow despite varied approaches; story called derivative [1].
- Over-ambitious scope forced content cuts and a 500-to-270 page design cut [1][6].
- AI left too late; conflicting two-team structure early in production [6].
- HR boss fights outsourced and criticised; Director's Cut added non-lethal options [3].
- MD: short campaign, microtransactions, subtle favouring of non-lethal play [4].
- PS2 port: long loads, frame drops, unimproved AI [1].

## Adaptation notes
- Limb health: 6 numbers per actor is cheap; show as HUD silhouette; use threshold bands (67% and 34%) for graded penalties [20].
- Augment draft: 4-6 passive/active perks with energy bar, per-minute drain (10 to 300 scale is a usable ratio) and exclusive pairs per slot.
- Inventory grid not needed; use loadout slots.
- Alarm propagation: radius broadcast of events with decay; no raycasts beyond line-of-sight checks.
- Skill ladder: 4 levels per weapon class with damage and spread multipliers; keep costs in tuneable table.
