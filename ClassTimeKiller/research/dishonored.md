# Dishonored (1 and 2)

Tags [n] refer to Sources; "unverified" = author recollection or not on a fetched page. "(snippet)" in Sources = only a search-result excerpt was read. Power costs for D1 come from Fandom wiki text seen only via search summaries [23].

## Identity
- First-person stealth action: sword/blade right hand, magic and ranged left hand [1]. Chaos system tracks violence rather than good/evil [1].
- Original stealth model was Thief-like light/shadow but was dropped because enemies ignoring a visible player felt unrealistic; designers also embraced unexpected power combinations found in testing [1][11].
- Arkane approach: give general-purpose tools instead of scripted set pieces, so the best moments emerge from player choice; do not shield players from awkward moments [11].

## Core loop
- Mission-based: infiltrate, pick lethal or non-lethal approach to target, collect runes/charms/coins, upgrade [1].
- Violence finishes faster but burns more health/mana potions [1]. Between missions the pub sells replenishable supplies [12].
- First playthrough about 10 to 15 hours per one guide [15].
- Dishonored 2: choose Emily or Corvo; NPCs randomly flagged sympathetic, guilty, or murderous each mission, with sympathetic kills adding the most chaos; the Heart reveals the flag [2].

## Movement
- Blink (short-range teleport): costs 20% of total mana; Tier I about 10 m, Tier II about 15 m (reaches farther sideways than up); can be chained as mana regenerates, ledge-climb possible when the climb arrow shows [15][22][23].
- Blink upgrades: Greater Blink (4 runes in D2 table) extends range; Redirective Blink (3 runes) stops time while aiming; Blink Assault (1 rune) knockdown/kill at arrival [7][22]. D1 rune cost for Blink Tier II: unverified (a fan site gives 3 for Corvo, 4 for Daud DLC) [22].
- Lean to peek and eavesdrop, keyholes, mantle (unverified). Running is audible, sneaking silent [18].
- Far Reach (D2): pull objects/enemies or clasp and travel; table: Pull Objects 2 runes, Decelerate 2, Pull Enemies 4 [2][7]. Shadow Walk (D2): shadow form, 4 runes; upgrades 2 (two targets), 1, 2 (faster), 2 (three targets) [7].

## Abilities and items
- D1 Corvo: Dark Vision, Blink, Possession, Devouring Swarm, Bend Time, Windblast (6 active), 4 passive enhancements (Vitality, Agility, Blood Thirsty, Shadow Kill), 40 bone charms [1]. Mana only partly regenerates automatically; potions restore the rest [15].
- D1 mana and runes (percent of mana, from wiki text via search summary, treat as probable) [23]:
  | Power | Mana | Runes / notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | Blink | 20% | see Movement |
  | Dark Vision | 20% (guide: very low; usable almost freely) | 1 rune tier I; sees guards, cones through walls; Great Dark Vision reveals objects/security; Premonition shows routes [7][12] |
  | Bend Time | 60% | tier I slows about 12 s; tier II stops time about 8 s; Stop Time listed as 8 runes, bought after Bend Time [12][23] |
  | Possession | high (about 60% per one wiki, unverified) | animals; human possession is a late upgrade; upgrades Chain Hosts, Lasting, Corpse [7] |
  | Devouring Swarm | high | 3 runes tier I, 4 tier II (unverified) [23] |
  | Windblast | 50% tier I (unverified, one guide says medium) | 3 runes tier I, 4 tier II [23] |
- Rune economy D1: up to 39 runes findable per playthrough, 58 needed for every power at tier II [snippet 24]. D2: 111 runes to max, 47 findable [24].
- D2 power table (rune cost; upgrades) from one guide; table text looked partly garbled, so treat as approximate [7]:
  - Bend Time 5; Lasting 2, Relativity 1, Stop Time 6. Possession 4; Chain Hosts 3, Lasting 1, Corpse 1, Human 4.
  - Devouring Swarm 4; Greater Swarm 2, Rat Piper 2, Twin Swarms 3. Windblast 4; Greater 3, Shockwave 3.
  - Domino 4 (Emily): links 2 foes, upgrades link 3 and 4 (3 runes each). Mesmerize 6: distracts 2, upgrades to 3 and 4 targets (3 each), Lasting 2.
  - Dark Vision 2; Great 3; Premonition 2.
- Mana costs for D2 powers are only given as qualitative (Possession and Bend Time very high; Windblast low; Blink low) [7][21]. Exact ranges (metres) for Windblast, Domino, Mesmerize, Far Reach: unverified.
- Bone charms: 35 in the main game but only 26 spawn per playthrough, randomized on level load (restart to reroll); 3 slots at start, up to 6 bought from Piero; per-mission spawn counts 1/5/5/3/2/2/8 [8]. Examples: Lucky Needle (recover sleep darts), Swift Shadow (faster creeping, stacks), Fading Light (free teleport soon after a kill), Void Favor/Surge (chance of free power), Witch's Skin (corrupted: damage drains mana first) [21][22].
- Emily D2: Far Reach, Mesmerize, Domino, Shadow Walk, Doppelganger (decoy) [2]. Corvo D2 keeps Blink, Swarm, Possession, Bend Time, Windblast; both share Dark Vision [2].

## Health and damage
- Health and mana elixirs, limited carry; sword and pistol lethal; sleep darts non-lethal (water or high ground can still kill the target) [1][17].
- Difficulty D1: four levels altering potion effectiveness, enemy awareness, damage and responsiveness; health regeneration on the easiest setting [1]. Easy reduces peripheral vision and slows detection [16]. No achievements tied to difficulty [15][16].
- D2: customizable difficulty added on 23 Jan 2017; also many found D1 too easy so hard settings were reworked [2].

## Modes and match options
- Dunwall City Trials DLC (11 Dec 2012): 10 challenge maps - Assassins Run, Back Alley Brawl, Bend Time Massacre, Bonfires, Burglar, Kill Cascade, Kill Chain, Mystery Foe, Oil Drop, Train Runner; four categories (mobility, stealth, action, puzzle); 10,000 points per star up to 30,000 for 3 stars; Expert versions unlock at 2 stars and usually add a restriction such as no powers; each star unlocks artwork [1][9]. Challenge types include wave survival, time trials and drop assassinations [1]. Example rules: Mystery Foe = find four clues unseen to identify the target; Burglar = steal six clockwork eggs and valuables (snippet) [25].
- D2 gained New Game Plus on 19 Dec 2016; the lack of replay at launch was called a possible deal-breaker by Polygon [2].
- Clone modes: ghost run (no kills, no alarm), high-chaos horde, challenge rooms with a power loadout and star scoring.

## Map design
- Layered verticality, rooftops/vents/windows; levels usually offer two or more routes; non-lethal solutions placed along the main path; objective markers can be turned off [1][18].
- Level design principles seen in Arkane statements: build many routes from general tools; avoid long tight winding passages that disorient; keep landmarks visually connected so players build a mental map; give rooms real-world functions players understand; AI navigation must work while the geometry changes (Clockwork Mansion) [11][19].
- Lady Boyle's Last Party: ideas from a whiteboard of mix-and-match concepts, contrast between plague streets and free movement inside; named a favorite level by Harvey Smith (snippet) [25].
- Also reported: guards rarely look up, so rooftops and ledges are safe [16][18].

## Bots and AI
- Awareness states: normal, suspicious, searching/alerted [1]. Visual indicator in D1: white bolts above heads; red and shattering means spotted (ends a ghost run) [16]. D2: a bar fills toward red; a quick flash means barely noticed, fuller means guards come to your last known spot, full red means alerted; drops after you kill witnesses or hide long enough (snippet) [13].
- RPG Codex: partial sighting makes a guard suspicious, a second sighting triggers alert; AI sharp horizontally but almost blind vertically; ignores open doors and stolen items; notices powered-off devices but never repairs them; may take over a missing comrade's patrol [18].
- Sound: running loud, sneaking silent, knocked items and bottles draw guards; a death scream can pull in a group; hidden player eventually returns guards to normal even with bodies visible [15][18]; D2 master AI assigns roles and coordinates reactions [2].
- Exact timers (seconds to forget, search duration, meter fill rate) and cone angles: unverified, no fetched page lists them.
- Chaos: hidden score, rises mostly in steps of 6 per civilian kill (fan wiki, via search); high rating when kills exceed about 20% of the mission's NPCs (fan guide, Steam users say up to 30%); more than 80% of humans including weepers must live for low chaos; rats, fish and game-spawned rat swarms do not count; weepers count as humans; level state for the finale uses the total going in; update can lag a mission [16][17].
- Effects: low = fewer rats/weepers, friendlier allies, small patrols; high = more rats and weepers, bloodflies in D2 corpses, heavier guard presence, darker weather and ending; three endings (low, medium, high) in D1 [1][2][14][18]. Stealth kills usually still lead to high chaos [15].
- Bot spec for clone: meter 0-100 per bot; fills by sight (distance x light x movement), noise and seen corpses; thresholds at suspicious / search / combat; searches last known position then area; share alert radius; ignore vertical (up) sightlines unless alerted.

## Progression
- Runes for powers, bone charms for passives, coins for gear [1]. The Heart locates runes and charms; revisit the pub hub as new ones open [12].

## HUD and options
- Minimal HUD; awareness bolts for guards; mana bar; selection wheel (unverified). Dark Vision shows cones; difficulty selectable; quest markers optional [12][18].

## Best features to keep
- Powers that combine (Domino + Windblast, Bend Time + sword) [1].
- Transparent awareness indicator and sound-based distraction [1][13].
- Non-lethal and lethal choice that changes world state [1].
- Cheap mobility (Blink) gated by mana regen rather than limited charges [22].

## Mistakes to avoid
- High chaos punishes violence with harder game and worse ending (Game Informer) [1].
- Inconsistent detection: spotting from far but missing close, ignoring doors and absent comrades [1][18]; reviewers found Blink overpowered and stealth charms making late game too easy [18].
- Too many options / powers trivialising exploration [1]; underused new enemy types, weak escalation, rushed story in D2 [2].
- D2 launch performance problems on PC and no NG+ at launch [2]. Length criticism unverified.
- Mission-level guidance too thin in some cases [1].

## Adaptation notes
- Blink: raycast to target, validate with capsule, 0.1 s dash with FOV flare; 10 m base, 15 m upgraded; 20% mana with regen.
- Possession: swap camera/control to a bot; low-cost.
- Bend Time: scale game dt for everything except player; slow 12 s / stop 8 s as tuning anchors.
- Chaos: global counter changes spawn counts and music; use percent-of-bots-killed threshold, ~20%.
- Guard cones: simple angle+distance+light-level checks; avoid per-frame full raycasts.
