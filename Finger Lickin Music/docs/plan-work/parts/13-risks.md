## 13. Risk register

This part is the risk register and severity classification demanded by E 12 (first bullet)
and by B 27 Phase 0 ("Identify technical risks"). It owns the `RISK-<nn>` IDs. Part 11 T
entries cite them in their "Open items" line; every phase gate re-reads this register
(0.4 step 5.2, procedure in 13.3).

At plan version 1.0.0 nothing is implemented, so every risk below is in state `open`
(13.3). The "Mitigation in design" column names the design elements that the implementation
must deliver; it does not describe existing code. A mitigation lowers no severity until the
row's verification has passed in a recorded run.

Columns of the register:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ID | `RISK-<nn>`, stable; never renumbered, reused or deleted (a retired risk keeps its row) |
| Severity | Critical, High, Medium or Low, assigned with the rules of 13.2 |
| Risk | what can go wrong, with its seed in the specification (E 1 row, E 10 item, other section) |
| Impact | the consequence for players, their work, or the delivery if the risk materializes |
| Mitigation in design | the plan elements that prevent or contain it: ADRs (Part 2), module designs (Part 6, cited `6.N` for module `M-NN`), contracts (Part 4), data rules (Part 5) |
| Early warning signal | an observable that shows the risk is materializing before users meet it: a test result, a log entry or event, a build report value, a counter |
| Phase that retires it | the phase (`Phase NN`, B 27 numbering 00-16) whose gate must show the verification passing; "re-checked" names a later phase whose gate repeats the check on more environments |
| Verification | test categories of Part 12 (UT, IT, FT, TT, PT, XT, FI, LT, AT) and, where Part 6 has already defined them, the unit test IDs; IT to LT IDs are assigned in Part 12; `review` means a documented inspection recorded in the LEDGER |

Seeds: RISK-01 to RISK-14 are the fourteen rows of E 1 in order, keeping their E 1
priority; RISK-15 to RISK-29 are the fifteen prohibited mistakes of E 10 in order (their
guards are also listed in Appendix C); RISK-30 to RISK-50 are notable cases of E 2 to E 9
not already covered by an E 1 or E 10 row; RISK-51 to RISK-77 are engineering and process
risks of this plan, including the three open questions OQ-1 to OQ-3 (RISK-53 to RISK-55).
Rows that say "decided here" add a small design element that the named module or screen
must carry; the module design and the Part 7.11 text catalog follow this register for it.

Fault names in the Verification column (`render.slowFrames`, `render.contextLoss`,
`timer.stall`, `audio.suspend`, `audio.fail`, `storage.quota`, `storage.writeFail`,
`import.truncate`, `input.disconnect`, `net.drop`, `net.delay`, `net.duplicate`,
`net.reorder`) are hooks of `FLM.debug.inject` (Part 4.8, list in Part 10.9). Invariants
`S-01` to `S-18` are those of Part 4.6.

### 13.2 Severity classification rules

Severity rates the worst credible impact if the risk materializes before its verification
has passed. It does not rate likelihood: before implementation there is no data to estimate
probabilities from, and a guessed probability would be an invented number (I 19 last
paragraph). Designing a mitigation never lowers a severity; recorded verification changes a
risk's state (13.3), not its severity.

| Severity | Assign when the worst credible impact includes any of | Consequence while the risk is open |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | (a) loss or silent corruption of saved user work or of a previous take; (b) musical timing drift in normal play, or a supported file losing or misordering musical events; (c) sound that persists after stop or a mode change; (d) the shipped file unable to perform a documented core function standalone, offline or from `file://` in a supported browser; (e) a rendering, audio or network failure that disables the transport, the audio or standalone play; (f) a false success or completion report: a save, an export, a requirement status or a compatibility claim; (g) execution of imported content, or exposure of credentials; (h) multiplayer accepting invalid authoritative results. Items (a) to (h) cover the ten E 13 release-blocking conditions and the B 26.8 criterion "no feature is falsely described as complete". | The gate of its retiring phase does not pass until the verification passes, except when the only missing evidence is an owner device test (the risk stays `open` with the note "awaiting owner device test" and the affected R-IDs stay Partial, 0.7 rule 3). At Phase 16 no Critical risk may be `realized` with its defect unfixed. |
| High | (a) a P0 or P1 feature fails for a whole browser, device class, input method or file category while the rest of the product works; (b) resource exhaustion that crashes, reloads or freezes the tab; (c) a misrepresentation of capability or fidelity in the UI or the documents; (d) a release-critical polish requirement (P 6) fails; (e) a process or tooling failure that would let defects pass a phase gate unseen. | Verified at its retiring gate. If the verification cannot pass there, the LEDGER records the reason and the next phase in which it can run; automated verification may not slip beyond Phase 15, device-dependent verification becomes an owner action. At Phase 16 an open High risk appears in the final report under unverified behavior. |
| Medium | (a) a P1 feature degrades while a workaround or fallback exists; (b) a Part 9.1 budget is missed while timing, data and core functions stay intact; (c) a P checklist item other than P 6 fails visibly; (d) a disclosure or usability problem without data loss. | Does not block a gate. At Phase 16 it is `retired`, or `accepted` and listed in the final report's known issues (B 28 item 11). |
| Low | a P2 feature, a cosmetic issue, or a process inefficiency with no effect on product quality. | Does not block a gate; `accepted` at Phase 16 if still open. |

Assignment rules:

1. Test the Critical list first, then High, then Medium; the first level with a matching
   item is the severity. When a reader can argue for two levels, the higher one applies.
2. A risk seeded from an E 1 row keeps at least the E 1 priority (Critical or High).
3. A risk whose materialization would make any E 13 condition true is Critical.
4. A severity changes only when the scope changes (the owner defers a requirement, or a
   feature is added). The change, with its reason, is recorded in the LEDGER "Decisions"
   section, and the register row is patched as a 1.0.x plan correction (0.1).
5. A realized risk becomes a defect row in STATUS.md section 6 with the same severity,
   unless the observed impact is smaller, which the defect row then states. Defects found
   without a matching risk are classified with the same table (0.7 rule 5).
6. Defect consequences: an open Critical defect stops the current T section (it cannot be
   marked done) and every phase gate until it is fixed and its regression test passes; an
   open High defect blocks the gate of the phase that owns the affected module and blocks
   Phase 16; Medium and Low defects do not block gates and are fixed or listed among the
   final report's known issues. Every fixed defect receives a regression test (I 17).
7. The severities in 13.1 were assigned with these rules at plan version 1.0.0.

### 13.3 Review cadence

Risk states. They describe risks, not requirements, and are written in lower case so that
they are never confused with the five requirement status words (0.9.2):

| State | Meaning | Allowed transitions |
|---|---|---|
| `open` | The mitigation is designed but its verification has not yet passed in a recorded run. Every risk is `open` at plan version 1.0.0. | to `retired`, `realized`, or (Medium and Low at Phase 16 only) `accepted` |
| `retired` | Every verification named in the row passed in a recorded run (command and result in the LEDGER) at or after the row's retiring phase. | to `realized` when a later `--all` run or device test fails that verification; the related requirements go back to Partial (0.7 rule 3) |
| `realized` | The risk occurred: an early warning signal was confirmed by a failing test or an observed failure, and a defect row exists in STATUS.md section 6. | to `retired` once the defect is fixed, its regression test passes and the row's verification passes |
| `accepted` | A Medium or Low risk still open at Phase 16, listed in the final report's known issues. Never used for Critical or High. | none |

When the register is reviewed:

1. Baseline (Phase 00). The first LEDGER entry (0.4 step 3) records "Risk register
   baseline: 77 open (Critical 17, High 41, Medium 19, Low 0)" and the result of RISK-72's
   plan validation.
2. Every phase gate (0.4 step 5.2). The full procedure below runs before the gate commit.
3. Event-driven, during any T section. When an early warning signal of any row is
   observed, the session reviews that row at once and writes one LEDGER line (RISK ID,
   signal, evidence). If a test failure confirms the signal, the row becomes `realized` and
   13.2 rule 6 applies before the T section continues.
4. Open questions. When the test that answers OQ-1, OQ-2 or OQ-3 runs (Part 2.19: Phase 15,
   Phase 14 and Phase 13), RISK-53, RISK-54 or RISK-55 is reviewed and the selected default
   or fallback is recorded where Part 2.19 says.
5. Pauses. Before a deliberate pause or on low context (0.4 step 6) no full review is run,
   but the `RESUME:` entry lists every risk that changed state since the last gate.
6. Final review (Phase 16 gate). Every row is reviewed. Release conditions: no Critical or
   High risk is `realized` with its defect unfixed; no Critical risk is `open` except those
   waiting only for an owner device test, which the final report lists as unverified; every
   still-open Medium or Low risk is `accepted` and listed among the known issues; the state
   counts appear in the final report (Part 14).

Gate review procedure:

1. Read every row of 13.1, including rows added by plan patches, not only the rows whose
   retiring phase is the current one.
2. Collect this gate's evidence: the `node tools/selftest.mjs --all` JSON, the
   `node tools/perf.mjs` JSON when the phase runs it, the build report (size against the
   RISK-60 envelope), log extracts from the test runs (`sched.stall`, `E-AUD-006`,
   `E-MODE-001`, `project.saveFailed`, `render.contextLost`, `net.message` with
   `valid:false`), and every XT and LT result recorded since the last gate.
3. For each row whose retiring phase is this phase: check every verification it names.
   Set `retired` when all passed; otherwise keep `open` with the reason and the phase in
   which the verification will run (13.2 table, High row), or set `realized`.
4. For each row whose "re-checked" phase is this phase: repeat its verification on the
   added environments; a failure sets `realized`.
5. For every other `open` row: compare its early warning signal with the evidence. A
   present signal is written to the review block; a confirmed one sets `realized`.
6. For each newly `realized` row: add the defect row to STATUS.md section 6 with severity
   and the regression test to be added, move the affected R-IDs to Partial or Blocked
   (0.7 rule 3), and apply 13.2 rule 6.
7. New risks found during the phase get the next free number (the numbers after RISK-77), all eight
   columns, a severity by 13.2, and are added to 13.1 as a 1.0.x plan patch with a LEDGER
   "Decisions" line. Rows are never renumbered or deleted.
8. Write the review block below into the gate's LEDGER entry and include it in the gate
   commit (0.4 step 5.5).

Review block format (one per gate LEDGER entry; rows only for risks whose state changed):
